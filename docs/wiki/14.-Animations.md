## Animations

You can build an animation in game and hand it to any NPC. No modelling software, no files to
install, nothing to type.

Open an NPC, go to the **Manage** tab, and press **Animations**.

---

### What an animation is here

A pose is the six body parts of the humanoid model, each turned and moved how you like. An animation
is a handful of those poses laid out on a timeline, and the game slides between them.

That is the whole idea. If you have used Blockbench, this will feel familiar. If you have not, it is
simpler than it sounds.

---

### The screen

**Left** is the animation as a whole: how long it runs, how fast, whether it loops, and the buttons
for the keyframe you are on.

**Middle** is a mannequin showing the moment you are looking at, with a faint **ghost** of the
previous keyframe behind it so you can see what changed.

**Right** picks a body part and sets its angles.

**Bottom** is the timeline.

---

### The timeline

Six lanes, one per body part. A mark in a lane means that part is keyed at that moment, so you can
see at a glance which limb moves when.

- **Click empty space** to add a keyframe there.
- **Drag the handles** underneath to move a keyframe in time.
- **Click a mark in a lane** to jump to that keyframe *and* select that part.
- **Left and right arrows** nudge the selected keyframe one tick at a time.

Keys snap to a tenth of a second. Hold **shift** for exact ticks, or for bigger jumps with the arrows.

Faint lines show where a key will land, with a brighter one every second. They thin out as you make
the animation longer, so a ten second animation does not turn into a wall of stripes.

---

### Posing

Pick a part on the right, then drag **X**, **Y** and **Z**.

**Rotate** turns the part. **Move** shifts it, which is what lets a head bob or a body lean rather
than just swivel.

You can also **click the limb on the mannequin and drag it**. Up and down swings it, side to side
turns it. Quicker than hunting for the right slider once you know the model.

---

### The bit that trips people up

**A keyframe only holds the parts you actually touch.**

Everything else slides between the nearest keyframes that *do* set it. A number in dark text is set
on this keyframe. A number with a **~** in front is being worked out from the keyframes either side.

This is a feature, not a quirk. It means an arm can swing across four keyframes while the head turns
on two completely different ones, and neither disturbs the other. In a tool where every keyframe held
a full pose, adding a keyframe to move the head would pin the arms wherever they happened to be.

**Clear this part** removes the selected part from the keyframe you are on, so it goes back to being
worked out.

---

### Easing

Every keyframe has one, and it changes how the animation *feels* more than anything else on the
screen.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Straight** | constant speed, mechanical |
| **Ease in** | starts slow, speeds up |
| **Ease out** | arrives gently |
| **Ease both** | slow at each end, quick through the middle |

New keyframes default to **Ease both**, because almost nothing in life moves at a constant speed. A
wave with easing reads as a person. The same wave without it reads as a robot arm.

---

### Length, speed and looping

**Length** is a box you type seconds into, with buttons beside it. Up to thirty seconds.

**Speed** multiplies it, from 25% to 400%, so one animation can be reused fast or slow without
rebuilding it.

**Loops** repeats forever. **Once** plays through and stops. On a loop the last keyframe blends back
round to the first, so there is no jump.

---

### Mirror

Swaps the left and right limbs and flips the sideways angles.

Build the left half of a walk cycle, copy the keyframe, mirror it, and you have the other half.

---

### Giving it to an NPC

Two ways, and they do different jobs.

**Always**, from the **Pose** tab. Click **Animation** to cycle through what you have written. The NPC
loops it forever. This is what you want for idles: a shopkeeper shifting their weight, a guard
scanning the road.

**Once**, from a reaction. Add a **Play animation** action to any trigger. The NPC plays it through
and drops back to its idle afterwards, so it can wave when talked to or flinch when hurt.

---

### Notes

- Writing animations is **admin only**. Everyone sees them.
- Up to 64 animations on a server, 32 keyframes each.
- Animations are saved with the world and sent to players when they join.
- **Blockbench models are separate.** A custom model animates from its own file through GeckoLib and
  is not affected by any of this. Everything here is for NPCs using the normal humanoid model.

### Playing an animation on a timer

On the **Pose** tab, under the idle animation, **Now and then** plays a second animation once on a
timer, then drops back to the idle loop. **Exact** fires on the beat, **Random** wanders. An Exact
animation and an Exact ambient sound set to the same gap land on the same tick, so a hammer swing
arrives with the clang. See [Sounds](15.-Sounds).
