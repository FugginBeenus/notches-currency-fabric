## Quests

A quest is a job an NPC hands out. You write it once, and after that any NPC can offer it by name.

Everything lives in one place: open an NPC, go to the **Manage** tab, and press **Quests**.

---

### Writing one

Type a name in the box at the bottom of the Quest Designer and press **+ New quest**. That opens the
quest itself.

**Goal** is what you are asking for. Click it to cycle through five:

| Goal | What the player does |
|---|---|
| **Kill** | kills a number of a mob |
| **Collect** | gathers a number of an item |
| **Talk to** | finds a named NPC and talks to them |
| **Go to** | walks to a spot |
| **Deliver to** | takes items to a named NPC |

The fields below change to suit. A *Kill* quest asks for a mob, a *Collect* quest asks for an item,
a *Go to* quest has a **Here** button that fills in wherever you are standing, and *Talk to* and
*Deliver to* have a **Pick** button that steps through the NPCs around you, so you never have to
spell a name.

Item and mob fields fill themselves in as you type. Start typing `whe` and the rest appears in grey;
press **Tab** or the **right arrow** to take it. Hover a filled field and it tells you the friendly
name, so `minecraft:wheat` reads back as *Wheat*. A red line under the field means no such thing
exists, and the quest will refuse to save rather than quietly turn into Air.

**Pays** is three boxes: coins, an item, and how many of that item. Use either, or both.

**Text** is what the player is told. Leave it empty and the quest writes its own line, like
*Take 3 Bread to Bob*.

---

### How a quest ends

The **Ends** button has two settings, and it matters more than it looks.

**Pays now** settles the moment the goal is met. Right for *go say hi to the blacksmith*, where
walking back would be a chore.

**Hand back** sends the player to an NPC to collect. Right for a story, because it gives you a scene
at the end.

You do **not** need to set anything up for a hand-back. Whoever gave the quest accepts it back, and
the player is told who to return to by name. If you want a *different* NPC to take it, put a
**Turn in quest** action on them.

*Collect* is always hand-back and the button is locked. Somebody has to receive the items.

---

### Chains

Two fields, and they do different jobs.

**Needs** is a gate. The quest exists but is refused until another one is finished. The player has to
find it themselves.

**Then** is a handoff. Finishing this quest starts the named one immediately, with no walking.

Both have a **Pick** button listing your other quests.

---

### Repeats

**Once only** or **Repeats**. A repeating quest comes back the next time you talk to the giver, not
instantly, so handing one in does not shove it straight back at you.

---

### Faction quests

**Who** switches between **Anyone** and **Faction**.

On Faction, only faction members can take the quest, and faction mates **within 48 blocks share the
progress**. Three people clearing a cave all count each other's kills. Everyone gets their own full
reward, not a split.

Leave the box beside it empty for any faction, or name one with **Pick** to restrict it further.

---

### Handing quests out

A quest does nothing until an NPC offers it. Two ways, and they suit different things.

**A reaction** is the quick way. Reactions → *When talked to* → **Give quest**, then **Pick** the
name. Good for a simple quest giver.

**A dialogue choice** is the proper way. Talk tab → Dialogue Studio, set a choice's action to
**Give quest**. Now the player accepts on purpose instead of being handed a job for walking past.

Use **Turn in quest** the same way when you want a specific NPC to receive it.

---

### One NPC, the whole story

This is where quests and dialogue meet, and it is worth setting up once to see it.

Give an NPC four pages and set each page's **Show if**:

| Page | Show if | Show if 2 |
|---|---|---|
| "Can you help me?" | Not started | |
| "Any luck yet?" | On quest | |
| "You found it!" | Ready | |
| "Thanks again." | Finished | |

The NPC opens on the first page that matches, so it says the right thing at every stage and the
opening line stops coming back once the player has taken the job.

A page can hold **two** conditions, which is what lets an NPC walk someone through a series. *Finished
the first quest* **and** *not started the second* is the page that says "now go see Jen".

---

### What the player sees

A tracker on screen shows what they are on and how far along. Long text wraps, and servers can change
its colours, move it, or turn its progress bar off in the config.

`/quests` opens their log. A finished quest has a **Hand in** button there as a safety net, in case
the NPC who gave it has been deleted.

Admins can run `/quests list` to see every quest written on the server.

---

### Notes

- Writing and editing quests is **admin only**. Anyone can play them.
- Quests never appear on the Bounty Board. Bounties are the board's random jobs; quests come from
  people.
- Quests and progress are saved with the world.
